# Start with the Combined Corpus Research AI

Use your usual AI assistant to explore the corpus collection, find relevant evidence, and prepare reproducible research. Start with one file. Add the corpus evidence relevant to your question when you are ready.

Prefer a folder containing the instructions and their original supporting files? [Download the ZIP edition](downloads/README.md), then extract it. Windows: right-click the ZIP and choose **Extract All**. macOS: double-click the ZIP. On iPhone or iPad, tap the ZIP in Files. The starter text file is inside the extracted folder.

For ongoing work, you can keep the file and your related research sources in a project. In ChatGPT, project sources and instructions are shared across that project's chats; start your research conversation inside that project. You can also begin in an ordinary chat. # Research prompts

Use these after the starter is loaded. Replace the bracketed details with your question. The assistant should identify accessible evidence before presenting findings.

## Find a starting point

> I am researching [topic/object/site]. Identify the relevant corpus projects and the files you can actually access. Propose three questions that those files could support, and explain what would require further evidence.

## Trace a statement

> Trace [statement] to the uploaded corpus evidence. Give record identifiers and exact source locators. Separate the recorded assertion from later interpretation and identify any shared upstream source.

## Compare editions

> For [object or inscription], show the recorded reading versions side by side. Retain damage, uncertainty marks, and segmentation differences. Explain whether their line or token units can be aligned. Do not choose a preferred reading unless the corpus records a justified preference.

## Understand coverage

> What does this corpus currently cover? Distinguish catalogue identifiers, physical objects, reading versions, encoded occurrences, and independently reviewed material. State the denominator where known and the limits where it is unknown. Use accessible current corpus evidence rather than the master directory's summaries.

## Run a reproducible calculation

> Using [file and field], calculate [measure] only if the corpus permits it. State the input version, inclusion/exclusion rules, unit, and method. Label the result as a new computation and provide enough information to reproduce it.

## Compare two corpora

> Compare [corpus A] and [corpus B] for [specific feature]. First check units, representation, source dependence, and uncertainty. If those do not support inference, give a descriptive comparison and explain the blocker. Do not infer shared sign values or language relationships from similarity.

## Turn a blocker into next steps

> What evidence, source acquisition, review, or methodological change is missing for [question]? Separate work we can complete from existing sources from decisions that require epigraphic expertise.

## Save a research record

> Prepare a short research log containing my question, files actually inspected, corpus versions/commits, record and source identifiers, methods, findings, uncertainties, and blocked claims. Include applicable attribution and rights notes. Mark missing information as unknown.

## Check the answer

> Audit your previous answer against the accessible files. Which claims have direct support, which are interpretations, and which are unsupported? Correct unsupported claims and identify any citations you could not verify.

# Cross-Corpus Comparability Matrix — 1.0

This matrix governs *types* of comparison. It does not pre-authorize any substantive historical or linguistic conclusion.

| Question type | Default | Requirement to proceed |
|---|---|---|
| Repository/evidence coverage | ALLOWED | Define each corpus's native counting unit |
| Provenance/rights architecture | ALLOWED | Compare policies/fields, not rights grants |
| Sign/string frequency | GATED | Compatible sampling units, exclusions and representation layers |
| Positional/structural pattern | GATED | Compatible segmentation/unit definitions and declared null model |
| Palaeographic similarity | GATED | Source-quality images/observations and explicit feature method |
| Phonetic/sign-value equivalence | BLOCKED by default | Independent positive evidence beyond shape/interoperability |
| Lexical similarity | BLOCKED by default | Established readings plus method controlling chance/borrowing/dependence |
| Genetic language relationship | BLOCKED by default | Accepted linguistic evidence and comparative method |
| Decipherment transfer | BLOCKED | Independent decipherment evidence; values from another script are not sufficient |
| Chronological inference | GATED | Independently supported dating/provenance |
| Independent replication | GATED | Demonstrated source/witness/method independence |

When requirements are unmet, descriptive comparison remains permissible if limitations are explicit.

# Corpus fleet benchmark — Linear A parity target

The corpus projects use Linear A as an engineering and research-governance benchmark, not as a linguistic source for other scripts.

Every member should converge on these capabilities where its surviving evidence permits:

1. **Coverage accounting** — explicit universe/denominator where knowable; discovery leads separated from admitted evidence.
2. **Source lineage** — stable object, record, source and locator identifiers; hashes for captured inputs; duplicates and derivative editions linked.
3. **Evidence layers** — observation/source transcription, normalization, computation and interpretation remain distinct.
4. **Admission gates** — machine-readable reasons for inclusion/exclusion; uncertainty and damage preserved.
5. **Independent verification** — source checking and human review recorded separately; shared witnesses never count as replication.
6. **Rights integrity** — source-specific permissions govern redistribution; public access alone is not permission.
7. **Reproducibility** — import/validation scripts, deterministic exports, current-status evidence and correction history.
8. **Negative results** — blocked, failed, quarantined and superseded analyses remain visible.
9. **AI authority** — individual skill indexes canonical status, evidence, rights and gates; generated state is rebuildable and never outranks corpus data.
10. **Cross-corpus safety** — Combined AI compares only semantically compatible units and never infers decipherment, sign values or linguistic relationship from interoperability.

## Parity rule

Parity means comparable rigor and research usability, not equal record counts. A corpus with five surviving inscriptions can meet the benchmark if those five are exhaustively identified, source-collated, provenance-complete, rights-clear, uncertainty-preserving and reproducibly validated. A corpus with hundreds of unverified rows cannot.

## Evidence acceleration

For every member, acquisition proceeds in this order: discover -> identify rights -> capture/hash or record locator -> parse without semantic invention -> reconcile object identity -> validate -> independently/source-check -> admit under declared policy -> regenerate AI bundle -> update Combined registry.

No maturity claim may be upgraded merely because more records were imported.

# Combined Corpus Research AI — Researcher Quickstart

This guide explains the research workflow after setup. For downloads and upload instructions, begin with [Start here](START-HERE.md). You can start with one text file, without Git or Python.

## Five-minute start

1. [Download the starter](downloads/README.md) and attach its text file to your AI conversation or project.
2. Use the first-message prompt in [Start here](START-HERE.md#3-send-this-first-message) to confirm that the assistant can read it and identify relevant corpora.
3. Choose a corpus from the [download directory](CORPUS-DOWNLOADS.md). Make its individual instructions, index, source-state and selected canonical evidence accessible. The starter and an index alone do not supply inscription evidence.
4. Ask your research question normally. [Prompts](PROMPTS.md) cover source tracing, edition comparisons and reproducibility.
5. For cross-corpus questions, require the AI to show the comparability check before interpreting similarities.

A good first prompt is: **“Which registered corpus projects are relevant to my question, what evidence can each currently provide, and which comparisons are allowed, gated or blocked?”**

## Good research questions

- “Find the evidence relevant to this sign or sequence and distinguish source observations from later interpretation.”
- “Compare positional patterns across these two corpora, but stop if their units or segmentation are not comparable.”
- “Trace this statement to corpus records and source locators.”
- “Reproduce this statistic and list its inputs and exclusions.”
- “Which apparent replications share an upstream source or witness?”
- “What specific evidence is missing before this blocked comparison could proceed?”
- “Show disagreements among editions without selecting a preferred reading unless the corpus records a justified preference.”

## What a trustworthy answer should show

For substantive work, expect the answer to identify the corpus project(s), evidence layer, relevant IDs/locators where available, evidentiary status, uncertainty/exclusions, source-independence limitations, reproducibility method for new calculations, and rights/attribution constraints when reproduction is involved.

For cross-corpus work, expect a **Comparability** section stating each corpus's unit of analysis, representation layer, source dependence and whether the requested inference is permitted.

## Undeciphered and poorly understood material

The system must not manufacture translations, phonetic values, morphemes or language relationships. Recurrence, position, visual resemblance or correspondence with a deciphered script may be reported as evidence only at the level actually established by the corpus.

A deciphered writing system also does not imply that every language written in it is understood. This is especially important for Eteocypriot.

## Deciphered corpora and controls

For Linear B, Cypriot syllabic Greek and other readable material, the system still separates transcription, normalization, restoration, linguistic interpretation and translation. Established readings from one script cannot be transferred automatically to another.

## When the AI says BLOCKED

Do not ask it to “take its best guess.” Instead ask:

**“What evidence, source acquisition, review, calibration or methodological change would be necessary to unblock this question?”**

That converts a negative result into a research agenda.

## Staleness and validation

Before relying on a generated bundle, inspect its source-state and research-bundle index. The source commit should identify the corpus state represented by the bundle. If generated material conflicts with canonical corpus files, canonical evidence wins and the discrepancy should be reported.

Individual corpus validators protect corpus-specific authorities. The Combined validator protects the registry and orchestration contract. Neither proves that an ancient reading is epigraphically correct or peer reviewed.

## Rights and citation

The Combined skill is not a licence umbrella. Every corpus and upstream source keeps its own terms. Ask the AI to identify the source and applicable rights before reproducing substantial source-derived material.

For scholarly citation, cite the underlying corpus record/source or publication rather than citing the Combined AI as though it were the evidence.

## Suggested reproducible workflow

**Question → route to corpus project(s) → validate bundle state → retrieve native evidence → classify evidence → check source independence → check comparability → compute only if permitted → report result and blockers → record corpus versions/commits and methods.**

This workflow is intentionally conservative. A scientifically meaningful BLOCKED result is preferable to a fluent unsupported conclusion.
